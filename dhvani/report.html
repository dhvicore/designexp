01 · Project overview

Syllabeat is an Android music-based learning prototype for NMIMS students. It combines curriculum-based educational songs, read-along lyrics and interactive activities in a single revision journey.

The project developed from a Calculus dataset and Python/Streamlit prototype into a Kotlin/Jetpack Compose Android app. The integrated curriculum now includes Calculus, Chemistry and Economics/Management. Calculus/Chemistry demonstrate nine saved Suno recordings. Economics adds on-device TTS-based song creation; high-quality singing for every subject remains unfinished.

Objective: make revision more engaging through songs, while checking understanding through activities. Improved marks or retention have not yet been established by a student study.

02 · Problem, SAT framework and research

Abstract formulas and definitions can be difficult to revisit through passive reading alone. The project investigates whether a familiar learning activity—listening to music—can become a useful route into structured revision.

  • Song: original topic lyrics organise key ideas into musical phrases and a chorus.
  • Activity: students retrieve facts through blanks, choices and sequencing.
  • Technology: an app connects the content, audio, read-along display and feedback.

The literature review considered music generation, language models for educational content and active learning. Freeman et al. (2014) provides evidence for active learning in STEM, but does not validate Syllabeat or prove that songs alone improve mathematics results. MusicGen research informed tool exploration, rather than serving as the finished Android singing engine.

Earlier reports contained pitch-accuracy and learning-benefit figures. Those are not asserted as current Android performance or outcome measurements here. A controlled student evaluation remains necessary.

Data collection covered subject syllabi, units, topic names, concepts, formulas or keywords, music categories and song references. 04 · What the Android app contains

Login and signup

Students create a local account with name, email and password, then log in. Input validation and duplicate-email checks are implemented. Accounts and the last successful email survive normal app reruns and in-place updates. The password must be entered again. This is device-local authentication, not institutional SAP/portal verification or cross-device login.

The five-step learning journey

  1. Learn: choose Calculus or Chemistry, then the unit and topic. Read the explanation and formula or key terms.
  2. Sound: select the supported category/language and a song reference. Recommendations depend on the topic; the relevant saved demo is prioritised when available.
  3. Lyrics: prepare a curriculum-based draft or load the words supplied for an existing recording. Review and edit the study draft.
  4. Listen: play the original saved MP3 offline, pause, replay, seek and read timed lyric lines. Tapping a timed line jumps to that point.
  5. Practice: complete the three activity types, review feedback and retry.

Branch, academic year and semester mappings await verified datasets. A complete saved-song library, history and cloud profile synchronisation are not part of the current finished demo.

UI/UX refinement

The interface uses cream, lime and lavender gradients, original forest-green music artwork, the bundled Outfit font, rounded cards and visible five-step navigation. A duplicated saved-song information card was removed to make the listening screen cleaner.

How a recording reaches the student

Syllabus facts + required conditions → original educational lyrics → human review → Suno website generation → downloaded MP3 → topic/category mapping → offline Android playback

The selected reference describes the intended musical direction; it is not proof that the recording reproduces a named commercial song's exact melody. Exact melody conditioning, licensed reference handling and consistently natural singing remain further work. No successful custom model training is claimed for this demo.

Example: Rolle’s Theorem. The learning content must include continuity on [a,b], differentiability on (a,b), and f(a)=f(b). Only then does the theorem guarantee at least one c in (a,b) with f′(c)=0.

Editing the displayed study draft does not alter a saved MP3. Topics without bundled recordings may use the experimental generation route when its external services are configured; the demo does not promise offline generation.

06 · Read-along lyrics and smooth playback

Each of the nine recordings has its own lyric timing cues. Desktop authoring tools read the unchanged audio and derive acoustic word/segment timings. Matching or forced alignment connects the supplied educational text to the recording. The exported start/end timestamps are bundled with the app; speech recognition does not run during playback.

The current line is highlighted using the player's actual position. Pause, replay and seeking therefore update the lyric highlight, and a student can tap a timed line to seek. The full supplied text remains available separately.

Timings are approximate for sung words. The approximately one-minute Rolle Hindi recording ends during its final refrain; words beyond the end of the file remain readable without invented playback timestamps.

Playback reliability work

The app moved to buffered Media3 playback, with a larger PCM output buffer and an ahead-of-playback loading buffer. The seek slider commits one seek when released, rather than repeatedly interrupting playback while dragged. Unnecessary redraw work was reduced.

When the original files played smoothly on the laptop but crackled in the emulator, diagnostics showed software graphics and audio timing spikes. The local emulator was switched to the Mac's GPU, its unused microphone input was disabled, and it was cold-booted without wiping data. This changed emulator settings, not macOS audio settings. Playback quality still depends on the host device and audio route.

07 · Activities, feedback and the theory prototype

The Android activity screen adapts three activity types from the earlier Python prototype:

  • Fill the blanks: recall important words from a topic's lesson.
  • Formula or key-term selection: choose the relevant Calculus formula or Chemistry keyword set from alternatives.
  • Order the lines: reconstruct the lesson or reasoning sequence.

The app provides answer feedback, a score out of three and retry. Activities work offline and are currently in English. Rolle and Beta contain more specifically prepared understanding checks, while other topics use content-derived activity data.

The earlier prototype also explored a Photosynthesis example and a Whisper-based “sing it back” task. Those were prototype explorations, not finished Biology content or microphone assessment in the Android app. Spaced-repetition reminders and progress tracking remain proposed Android additions.

Saved-song playback does not send the student's account details to a generation service. Experimental generation uses musical parameters and topic lyrics and requires an external runtime. Temporary keys, local configuration, model weights and generated research output are excluded from the repository.

Uninstalling the app, clearing its data or wiping the emulator removes local accounts. Normal in-place updates preserve them. The app does not currently provide password recovery, enterprise security certification, cloud account sync or institutional authentication.

Economics/Management integration · November update

Krishhma Vira’s contribution from dhvicore/design-experience adds 32 topics in seven units: Introduction, Market Structure/Macroeconomics, Functions of Central Bank, Theory of Production, Introduction to Management, Organizing and Controlling. Descriptions, keywords and song suggestions support English, Hindi and Marathi. Twelve topics contain supplied English lyrics; remaining lyric drafts use templates grounded in the localized content.

The Economics route uses no Suno or Colab API. Android TextToSpeech records each line. The imported pitch tracker, syllable splitter, PSOLA renderer and beat composer arrange the voice into an original melody with accompaniment. EconomicsAudio.kt saves a PCM WAV and line cues. The existing buffered Media3 player handles playback and timed lyrics. Installed voices are preferred, but network voices may need internet. Missing voices produce an error rather than an instrumental-only success.

The first Economics topic generated and played in English, Hindi and Marathi during technical checks. Twenty-eight targeted instrumentation tests passed for Economics, activities, accounts and Chemistry. These checks establish implementation behaviour, not natural singing quality or learning effectiveness. Song names guide style and tempo, not verified reproduction of commercial melodies.

Short code explanation

SongStudio.kt controls the five screens. Curriculum.kt and subject adapters load the JSON data. AccountStore.kt handles persistent local accounts and password hashes. EconomicsAudio.kt creates and caches Economics audio. LocalSongEngine.kt uses Media3 for buffered playback. TimedLyrics.kt uses the playback clock to highlight lyric cues. LearningActivity.kt creates and checks blanks, formula/key-term choices and ordering tasks.

09 · Development journey and tool decisions

  1. Concept and logbook: identified the revision problem, proposed SAT and documented the original semester work.
  2. Dataset preparation: collected Calculus topics, formulas, applications, song types and reference mappings.
  3. Python/Streamlit prototype: explored TTS, pitch/length shaping, simple melody playback, activity generation and a theory-topic demonstration. Robotic singing was an important limitation.
  4. AI tool exploration: researched MusicGen and MagicSchool, generated songs on Suno, and tested an experimental Colab/ACE-Step route. Generation, precision, connection and vocal-quality issues prevented treating the experimental route as a finished reliable service.
  5. Android implementation: built local login/signup, curriculum navigation, category choices, lyric review, playback and practice. Added the Chemistry catalog alongside Calculus.
  6. Demo packaging: integrated eight Calculus recordings and the Dalton English recording, then added read-along timings and player checks.
  7. Frontend and reliability: refined typography, gradients and navigation, simplified the listening screen, increased buffering and corrected emulator configuration.
  8. Delivery: committed the implementation and README to GitHub and prepared a project presentation, report and speaking script.

The team shared subject research, dataset preparation and song references. This report records the integrated work rather than asserting that every originally assigned workstream is complete.

10 · Testing evidence and current limitations

Recorded checks included Android build/lint, all-topic curriculum loading, stable mappings, local-account persistence, quiz content/answer handling, native MP3 decoding, playback continuity, seek/replay and lyric cue ordering/boundaries. A dedicated test exercised the actual buffered Media3 engine across all nine bundled recordings without reported audio underruns in that run.

These are technical checks, not proof of exact melody fidelity, perfect pronunciation or a learning benefit. A zero-underrun test cannot guarantee every user's speakers, Bluetooth route or emulator will be crackle-free.

Proposed evaluation

Recruit consenting student volunteers and compare conventional revision with song-plus-activity revision on matched topics. Use the same immediate and delayed quizzes, review conceptual errors and accessibility, and report uncertainty rather than claiming an improvement in advance.

11 · Future innovations and expansion

  • All subjects: integrate Physics, Biology, Economics and further approved syllabus datasets using the shared schema.
  • Academic hierarchy: support verified branch → year → semester → subject → unit/topic selection.
  • More finished content: create and review recordings for more topics and add more regional languages.
  • Teacher review: introduce content approvals, formula/condition checks, pronunciation review and correction workflows.
  • Supported singing integration: connect a suitable API with job status, retries and quality validation; explore licensed melody conditioning rather than claiming current exact-song control.
  • Personal library: add saved songs, history, secure cloud storage, cross-device accounts and progress continuity.
  • Adaptive practice: add topic-level progress, spaced repetition, revision reminders and explanations for mistakes.
  • Sing-back: investigate speech/word feedback and pronunciation support, building on the earlier prototype.
  • Institutional integration: pursue SAP/college portal login only with authorised access from the institution.
  • Evaluate and improve: conduct student usability and retention studies before making outcome claims.
Conclusion: Syllabeat demonstrates a complete Android revision journey with structured curriculum content, educational songs, read-along lyrics and active practice. The next phase is broader content, more reliable generation and measured educational value.
